  - **Purpose:** Evaluate whether this free, offline, non-diagnostic questionnaire helps hesitant users organise experiences and feel more comfortable considering a conversation with a mental-health professional. The app does not diagnose, score symptoms, interpret results, recommend treatment, or determine whether care is needed. Assess the *existing* application, not an idealised commercial product; avoid perfectionism and unnecessary features.
  - **1 — User motivation/help-seeking:** Inspect the introduction, navigation, section/completion flow and final report preparation. Is the optional next step toward a consultation clear and approachable? Are there discouraging barriers? Do not add pressure tactics or diagnostic interpretations. Keep this distinct from the separate wording audit.
  - **2 — Questionnaire usefulness/coverage:** Inspect actual questions, answers, sections and branching for meaningful omissions, duplicates, mismatched choices, hidden relevant questions, poor organisation or excessive low-value burden. Do not suggest new questions solely to increase coverage, and preserve the significance of sensitive questions.
  - **3 — Completion barriers/usability:** Inspect questionnaire length, optional sections, progress, skipping, backtracking, autoswitch/selection behaviour, save/restore and partial completion; identify realistic abandonment risks, confusing transitions and unnecessary taps.
  - **4 — Clinician PDF usefulness:** Examine whether answers and question context are reproduced accurately; unanswered/skipped items are excluded as intended; concerning responses are identifiable without diagnostic interpretation; counts are correct; printing and organisation support quick review without obscuring important content.
  - **5 — Technical reliability:** Inspect branching, single/multi-select, navigation, skipping, language switching, progress calculations, manual save/restore, incomplete sessions, export/report generation, mobile browser compatibility and error handling. Existing non-destructive tests may be run; do not create new test files. Distinguish executed/verified defects from code-inspection risks.
  - **6 — Privacy, accessibility and safety:** Check whether real data handling matches privacy claims; report export/storage risks; usability for people with limited technical experience; readable/mobile-accessible controls; appropriate, proportionate emergency information; material legal/privacy/accessibility concerns. Avoid repetitive disclaimers or needless infrastructure.
  - **Evaluation standard:** Classify each finding as **Critical** (serious verified issue or substantial risk), **Moderate** (meaningful reduction in completion/trust/report usefulness), **Minor** (genuine low-impact issue), or **Optional** (subjective enhancement, not a defect). Do not present hypothetical issues as confirmed facts; a difference from commercial product design is not automatically a defect.
  - **For each finding:** State category, file/function/section/question ID where applicable, evidence, what was observed, verified vs inferred status, severity, expected benefit (High/Medium/Low), implementation effort (Small/Medium/Large), and whether it warrants attention *before* public release. Qualitative benefits are reasoned estimates, not measurable changes in appointment rates or claims of clinical validation.
  - **Final report:** Summarise release readiness; count findings by six categories and severity; list the five strongest benefit/effort opportunities; identify real release blockers vs deferrable improvements; note already-sufficient areas that should be left untouched, testing gaps and limitations; choose **Ready / Ready after targeted fixes / Significant work required / Insufficient evidence**. Explicitly answer whether changes are likely to meaningfully improve completion, accurate report preparation, or comfortable consideration of speaking with a professional.
  - **Resource efficiency:** Review existing code, documents, tests and previous audit results before further work; avoid repeating the wording audit or undertaking extensive new research without a material reason. Provide concise, actionable findings. **Make NO changes as part of this audit; wait for owner approval.**
